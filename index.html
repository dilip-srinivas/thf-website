Our Approach • 2026
"For years, we raised concerns with district authorities while traditional contour terraces were cleared, social forests were encroached upon and stream banks were affected by silt and agricultural runoff. Where the public voice was strong, as at Longwood Shola, plans were reconsidered. Where changes happened plot by plot, our representations often went unanswered. We now organise our field evidence, RTI responses and survey maps into clear, well-documented initiatives, so that authorities have what they need to act."
Our standing comes from years of presence in the field: working alongside the Forest Department, documenting concerns carefully and following up with the authorities.

Community Conservation Campaign • Kotagiri Taluk
November 2023 – 2024

Longwood Shola: Plan for a 6,000 Sq. Ft. Building Inside the Reserve Forest Withdrawn

Between November 2023 and 2024, we joined residents in a sustained campaign of ecological representations and public meetings regarding a proposal to build a 6,000 sq. ft. concrete interpretation centre inside Longwood Shola Reserve Forest, Kotagiri's high-altitude urban shola and an important micro-watershed. After public engagement and a clear presentation of the hydrological risks, the state government withdrew the proposal for construction inside the forest boundary.

Documented Ground Outcomes
  • The 6,000 sq. ft. building inside the Reserve Forest was cancelled.
  • The core watershed that supplies water to over 30,000 residents of Kotagiri was protected.
  • Wider citizen interest grew, supporting better protection for the Ramsar wetland site.

Wildlife Protection Support • Forest Corridors
2022–2025

Supporting the Forest Department Against Wildlife Snaring

Between 2022 and 2025, we carried out field observation along tea-estate and Reserve Forest border corridors and documented active wire-snaring. We shared geo-stamped evidence with the Forest Department's flying squad, which led to an on-site raid and the registration of a case under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

Documented Outcomes
  • Hazardous clutch-wire snares were removed from the field.
  • A case was registered under Sections 9 and 51 of the Wildlife Protection Act.
  • Routine volunteer de-snaring patrols were established along migratory paths.
Forest Boundary Care • Reserve Forest Perimeters
2021–2025

Reserve Forest Buffer Zone and Tree-Felling Concerns

Between 2021 and 2025, we documented unpermitted construction near Reserve Forest boundaries and unauthorised tree-felling on steep slopes, and submitted detailed representations to the District Collector and District Forest Officer. These led to spot inspections and stop-work notices.

Documented Outcomes
  • Forest Rangers inspected 8 locations along contested boundaries.
  • Heavy-machinery levelling within critical forest buffer strips was halted.
  • Our field documentation was compiled into a record now available for further action.
Watershed Care • Revenue Watersheds
2021–2025

Social Forest, Grazing Commons and Riverbank Concerns

Between 2021 and 2025, we followed a multi-year process of representations on the use of government social-forestry plantations, revenue grazing commons and riverbanks across Kotagiri and Ketti Valley. By tracing historical village survey maps, we documented how public waterways were being diverted.

Our Restoration Campaign • Ongoing
2020–Present

Ecological Restoration with Forest First (forestfirst.org)

Since 2020, our Forest First campaign has been restoring degraded montane shola-grasslands and riparian wetlands across the Nilgiris. We clear invasive black wattle and lantana and grow native endemic saplings, restoring water balance and reducing the risk of landslides.

THF/LEGAL/NIL-EM/2026/001 ACTIVE • RTI APPLICATIONS SENT BY RPAD (26.10.2026)
Kotagiri Coonoor Udhagamandalam Geological Risk & Slope Conservation
Initiated: 15-10-2026

Regulating Mini-Excavators (15–35 HP) on Hill Slopes, and Protecting Traditional Contour Terraces

What Happened & Why It Matters Heavy rain of 200–250 mm across the Kotagiri and Coonoor ridges on 22–24 October 2026 led to landslides, slope failures and mudflows. Many of these areas had recently been altered by small excavators (15–35 HP) cutting steep vertical faces into fragile tea and agricultural terraces without the required permissions.

Pillar 01 Severe Erosion

Intensive Root-Crop Monoculture & Terrace Destruction

Replacement of centuries-old contour terraces with leveled fields for 3-cycle commercial crops (carrots, beets, lettuce), triggering massive monsoon topsoil erosion and chemical leaching into aquifers.

Legal Basis: Disaster Management Act 2005 (Sec 30/34); TN Land Improvement Schemes Act.
Proposed Remedy: Mandatory contour terrace preservation, ban on downslope furrowing, soil testing audits.
Pillar 02 Slope Stability

The 20–30 HP Mini-Backhoe Loophole

Small earthmovers brought in under the guise of "farm work" (02.03.2021 Horticulture circular) used to bulldoze agricultural terraces and cut unstable vertical slopes (>1:3 gradient) for real estate.

Legal Basis: Madras High Court August 2012 Earthmover Ban Order; Collector Directive 02.03.2021; W.P. No. 25913 of 2022.
Proposed Remedy: District AIS-140 GPS telemetry tracking, Agri-Engineering site vetting, spot impoundment.
Pillar 03 Corridor Severance

Hill Building Code Violations & Absentee "Ghost Villas" in Wildlife Corridors

Holiday homes occupied for only a short time each year are sometimes built beyond the 7-metre height cap, the 1:3 slope rule and the 30 m geological buffer, and are fenced across wildlife corridors, which adds to human-animal conflict.

Legal Basis: TN District Municipalities (Hill Stations) Building Rules 1993; TNCDBR 2019; Wildlife Protection Act 1972.
Proposed Remedy: AAA/HACA sanction audit, demolition of high compound walls (>2.4m) and energized solar fences, sealing stilted villas.
Pillar 04 Groundwater Stress

Wetland Conversion & Borewell Aquifer Depletion

High-altitude marsh sponges converted into commercial farms; while new borewells face paper restrictions, existing borewells pump unchecked alongside commercial carrot-washing units, sucking the water table dry.

Legal Basis: Wetlands Rules 2017; Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act 1974 (Sec 24/25/33A); Ground Water Guidelines.
Proposed Remedy: Compulsory 3-stage closed-loop silt-traps, mandatory telemetric flow meters and extraction caps on existing borewells.
Pillar 05 Habitat Pressure

Reserve Forest Buffer & Riverbank Encroachment

Illegal construction in RF buffer zones, encroachment of social forests and stream margins, and unauthorized tree felling.

Legal Basis: Tamil Nadu Forest Act 1882 (Sec 21); TNPPF Act 1949; Supreme Court directives in T.N. Godavarman.
Proposed Remedy: Joint Revenue-Forest GIS demarcation of boundaries, resolution of buffer-zone encroachments, and action against unauthorised tree-felling.
Pillar 06 Bird & Wildlife Haven

Acoustic & Ecological Shield for Migratory Birds (Total Firecracker Ban)

Unlike plains districts, over 60% of the Nilgiris District is forest, and almost every settlement—especially secluded absentee vacation villas and resorts—sits directly on a forest fringe or wildlife corridor.

Alongside our annual district-wide awareness campaigns during the peak October–November winter bird migration season, The Heritage Foundation has formally petitioned the Nilgiris District Administration for a Total District-Wide Ban on Firecrackers, and is now escalating this demand into statutory enforcement (Docket THF/LEGAL/NIL-FC/2026/007).

Legal Basis: Noise Pollution Rules 2000; Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Sec 9 & 39); Sec 144 CrPC / Sec 163 BNSS; Environment Protection Act 1986.
Proposed Remedy: A statutory notice and RTI application were sent by RPAD on 26.10.2026 to the District Collector, Field Director (MTR), DFOs, TNPCB and SP Nilgiris, requesting prohibitory orders under Sec 163 BNSS and enforcement of noise-pollution norms in the festival season.

Our Restoration Campaign

Forest First: Ground-Level Ecological Regeneration

Protecting what remains and restoring what has been lost go hand in hand. Through our Forest First campaign, we do hands-on restoration of degraded shola-grassland patches and riparian wetlands across the Nilgiri highlands. By removing invasive black wattle and replanting native endemics, we help stabilise slopes and reduce the risk of future landslides.
