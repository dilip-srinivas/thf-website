Landmark Civic Agitation Kotagiri Taluk • Shola Ecosystem
November 2023 – Early 2024

Halting the Proposed 6,000 Sq. Ft. Concrete Interpretation Center Inside Longwood Shola Reserve Forest

Longwood Shola is Kotagiri’s natural jewel—a pristine 116-hectare montane wet temperate forest recognized internationally as a Ramsar Wetland Site and the vital urban micro-watershed supplying perennial drinking water to over 30,000 residents.

The Administrative Proposal & Threat

In late 2023, the Tamil Nadu Forest Department proposed the construction of a heavy 6,000 sq. ft. concrete eco-tourism and interpretation center sited directly inside the core Reserve Forest boundary. The project entailed pouring deep reinforced concrete footings, trenching across ancient tree root systems, and altering fragile subsurface hydrological channels feeding town aquifers.

Our Grassroots Resistance

The Heritage Foundation spearheaded sustained ground agitations, mobilized Kotagiri citizens, environmental scientists, and indigenous elders, and submitted hard-hitting technical representations to district authorities. We demonstrated that heavy concrete infrastructure inside a Ramsar shola violates the Forest (Conservation) Act and threatens the town’s primary drinking water supply.

Documented Public Victory
  • Government Drops Construction Inside Forest: Confronted with relentless public protests and technical scrutiny, the Forest Department formally withdrew the proposal to build the concrete structure inside Longwood Shola Reserve Forest.
  • Canopy & Aquifer Intact: The ancient canopy, dense moss beds, and deep hydrological recharge system were protected from mechanical damage and heavy foundation excavation.
  • Community Precedent: Proved that vigilant, well-substantiated citizen action can halt unscientific state-sponsored construction within designated forest reserves.

Wildlife Offense Case Registration Kotagiri / Coonoor Interface
2022–2025

Covert Anti-Poaching Surveillance & Perpetrator Apprehension

The fringe boundaries between commercial tea estates and Reserve Forests in Kotagiri are frequent hotbeds for illegal wildlife snaring. Clutch-wire snares set for wild boar and barking deer indiscriminately maim and kill endangered leopards, Indian gaurs, and civets.

Field Action Timeline

Following tip-offs from estate workers, our ground volunteers conducted stealth reconnaissance along active wildlife paths. We geolocated active snare lines, tracked the poacher's cache, and alerted the Tamil Nadu Forest Department flying squad. By providing exact coordinates and real-time surveillance, forest officers executed a surprise raid, apprehended the suspect on site, confiscated illicit implements, and registered an official Wildlife Offense Case under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

Statutory Relevance for Court Submissions
  • Establishes organizational competence in gathering forensically sound, geocoded wildlife crime data.
  • Demonstrates ongoing cooperative engagement with state enforcement machinery, confirming bonafide public interest intent.
Buffer Zone Surveillance Nilgiris Eastern Slopes
2021–2025

Reserve Forest Buffer Zone Surveillance & Tree-Felling Representations

Under Supreme Court directives in the landmark T.N. Godavarman judgments and Tamil Nadu planning laws, a mandatory 100-metre protective buffer zone exists around Reserve Forests where commercial developments and unpermitted tree-felling are strictly regulated.

Our team conducted extensive ground audits exposing resort owners cutting access roads and clearing native canopy trees along reserve forest edges under the cover of night. We formally filed repeated representations with the District Collector and DFO, documenting:

  • Felling of mature native shola species without Timber Transit or District Tree Authority permits.
  • Earth excavation altering natural hill gradients within 50 metres of forest markers.
  • Discharge of graywater and septic overflow from unauthorized luxury villas into forest streams.
Transition to Legal Docket: While several spot notices were served by rangers, systemic enforcement continually stalled due to lack of coordinated inter-departmental action. These unresolved petitions have been codified into Docket THF/LEGAL/NIL-BF/2026/003 now awaiting listing before the Madras High Court Special Forest Bench.
Revenue Commons Defense Kotagiri & Ketti
2021–2025

Social Forestry & Poramboke Stream Bank Encroachment Petitions

In the Nilgiris, government social forestry reserves and revenue *poramboke* (unassessed public lands) perform an indispensable hydrological duty—they serve as natural drainage swales and sponges that absorb torrential monsoonal rains.

Through our archival research of historical British survey maps and village *adangal* registers, we identified critical parcels where private landowners had physically diverted perennial spring courses, filled in natural swamps (*vavus*), and erected concrete walls across drainage waterways. Our continuous petitions triggered administrative notices by the Revenue Tahsildar and prevented the permanent obliteration of two key natural stream corridors.

Sister Field Initiative Ecological Science & Regeneration
2020–Present

Hands-on Native Ecosystem Restoration with Forest First (forestfirst.org)

Legal enforcement without ecological restoration leaves hills vulnerable to slope collapse. Through our partner non-profit, Forest First, we execute the systematic physical restoration of degraded shola-grassland mosaics across Kotagiri and the upper Nilgiri plateau.

Restoration Highlights
  • Eradication of invasive alien black wattle (Acacia mearnsii) and Cestrum that dry out mountain water tables.
  • Propagation of endemic native shola saplings (Syzygium, Litsea, Rhodomyrtus) in dedicated high-altitude nurseries.
  • Replanting native tussock grasses along steep landslide scarps to bind topsoil and re-establish root tensile strength.

Pillar 6: Acoustic & Ecological Shield for Migratory Birds & Total Firecracker Ban

Unlike plains districts, over 60% of the Nilgiris District is forest, and almost every settlement—especially secluded absentee vacation villas and resorts—sits directly on a forest fringe or wildlife corridor.

Alongside our annual district-wide awareness campaigns during the peak October–November winter bird migration season, The Heritage Foundation has formally petitioned the Nilgiris District Administration for a Total District-Wide Ban on Firecrackers, and is now escalating this demand into statutory enforcement (Docket THF/LEGAL/NIL-FC/2026/007).

Statutory Demands & Conservation Ground Rationale
  • Prevention of Wildlife Stampedes & Human Fatalities: Concussive aerial detonations (>110–135 dB) cause acute panic among large mammals (Indian Gaur, Asian Elephants, Leopards), driving herds in blind terror out of forest borders into tea-worker lines and road traffic.
  • Protection of Migratory Avian Flyways: Thousands of exhausted Himalayan and Palearctic migratory birds arriving in the October–November window suffer fatal acoustic trauma, panic night-flight collisions, and permanent shola roost abandonment.
  • Montane Wildfire Risk Elimination: Exploding pyrotechnics and falling aerial flares pose an extreme fire hazard to dry frost-bitten shola-grassland perimeters on inaccessible gradients.
  • Pre-Litigation 7-Day Statutory Notice: Formal notice served under Section 144 CrPC / Section 163 BNSS upon District Magistrate and Forest authorities ahead of Deepavali (08.11.2026), with emergency escalation to the Madras High Court Special Forest Bench.
